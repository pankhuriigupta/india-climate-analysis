Insights

  • Warming is steady and accelerating: the trend is about 3x steeper since 1975, and the early-period fit is weak (R² 0.11), so the acceleration is the more reliable signal.
  • Cold-season warming leads: February (+1.67 °C), March (+1.37 °C) and December (+1.24 °C) warmed most; June (+0.50 °C) least.
  • Rainfall average is stable, variability is not: the monsoon share stays near 75%, but deficient years cluster in some decades (1900s: 5, 1910s: 4, 2000s: 3).

Conclusion

India's national climate shows a clear, accelerating temperature rise but no significant change in average rainfall. For agriculture and water planning, the risk is heat concentrated in the cold season plus year-to-year monsoon swings, not a falling rainfall average. Regional (state-level) data is the next step, since national averages can hide local change.
